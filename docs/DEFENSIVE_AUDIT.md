# Defensive fundamentals audit

Branch baseline: `55bf325` on `main`. This is a correction to the existing Phase 2A generator; the SVG, timeline sampler, playback controls, runner path system, and 24-play encyclopedia remain shared and intact.

## Convention basis and scope

The adopted system follows the user's supplied **Ball, Base, Backup** conventions, the provided **Perry Hill’s Infield System — Training Manual** by Perry ‘Bone’ Hill, and the repository's youth-baseball assumptions. The user explicitly authorized proceeding without the blocked coaching websites. Baseball Positive, Pro Baseball Insider and Ducks Baseball were suggested for further review; their coaching articles were **not retrieved or verified in this environment**. This report does not quote or attribute instructions to those sources.

This is one coherent teaching system for junior-high players, not a claim that every team must use these assignments. Coaches should review the cutoff personnel, optional catcher support, positioning depths and timing margins against their own manual. The conventions and capabilities are centralized in `src/dynamic/conventions.ts`.

Adopted conventions:

- Field the ball first, cover a live receiving/runner threat next, then allocate backups and support. An empty base already passed by every relevant runner does not automatically reserve an infielder. Second remains a control base on an outfield return and while a ground-ball play at first develops.
- On an infield play at first, P backs up the throw unless needed to receive there or to back an imminent home/third play. When 1B fields away from first, P has first call on receiving at first.
- C protects home and directs the defense. The default optional `catcherBackupFirst` convention also sends C up the first-base foul side when no immediate scoring threat exists. A scoring attempt, a runner near third, or an established runner at third retains plate coverage. Turn the convention off to keep C home on routine grounders. C returns toward the catching position after the supporting job ends.
- A neighboring outfielder backs up the batted ball. On a routine ground ball to 2B, this is usually RF; after fielding, RF can back the throw to first. For a left-field hit, CF supports LF while RF supports second. The opposite pattern applies on right-field hits. At fielding/reception, an available outfielder nearer the actual receiving lane can take the primary throw-backup job; an actual relay reception can require backup before speculative secondary support; the far outfielder can provide a separate, deeper layer.
- At second, the ball-side middle infielder is the cutoff and the other middle infielder receives. SS cuts throws to third; 1B cuts throws home. Existing coverage is retained when it makes the receiver ready sooner. If both middle infielders have higher-priority responsibilities, RF/CF can cover second before recalling a distant emergency infielder.
- Cutoff personnel and positions follow the selected target. The cutoff is on the source-to-target line, currently 55% of the distance from the outfielder. This fixed fraction is a documented geometric simplification, not an ideal depth for every arm or field.
- A shorter outfield throw can pass **through** a ready cutoff to the base. A long relay actually changes possession and consumes a transfer; a coach-selected cutoff-and-hold stops at that player. A relay receiver reevaluates the target and may redirect or hold. There is at most one intermediate relay in the current model.
- After BR passes first by more than the schematic lead allowance, 1B can trail outside the first-to-second running lane when first is no longer threatened and 1B is not receiving, cutting, or covering a live base. Trailing stays behind BR with about four metres of lateral separation; it is not an unconditional instruction to leave first.
- Starting positions hold R1 at first when second is unoccupied; use middle-infield double-play depth with R1 and fewer than two outs; use infield-in under the explicit prevent strategy with R3 and fewer than two outs; and use halfway depth in a close game from inning four under standard strategy. Two outs remove the run-prevention infield-in/halfway adjustment. Conservative strategy retains deeper outfield positions. These are team choices, not rules of baseball.

**Deliberate adaptations:** This app uses 1B as the home cutoff in all sectors, retaining 3B at third and compensating at first/second. The manual instead uses 3B as the home cutoff for certain left-field singles (p. 57), and different middle-infielder personnel for some right-side throws to third (pp. 62–63). Those are coordinated alternate systems; the app does not silently mix their personnel. It also keeps C at home on routine outfield returns, whereas the manual follows the hitter on bases-empty singles (pp. 51–53); the user-authorized optional first-base support here applies to infield throwing plays. P's routine first-base backup comes from the user's supplied convention, not an attribution to the manual.

The manual's double-cut backup around 30 feet behind the relay (pp. 31/60–67) is an organized second relay role. The app's generic backup behind one intermediate receiver is **not** claimed to implement that double-cut system. Likewise, the Six Fs' physical follow-through (pp. 4–11), receiving footwork and exact cutoff depth are not modeled by the point markers. These distinctions avoid presenting a partial visual model as complete instruction from the manual.

## Findings and underlying corrections

| Finding in the baseline                                                                                          | Reusable correction                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Reserving first, second, third and home on every play consumed C/P before backup allocation.                     | Derive live-base needs from runner progress, goal, retreat and status; protect home threats explicitly and release irrelevant bases.                                                                                                                       |
| Initial coverage was often built around a provisional second-base target regardless of the developing play.      | Anticipate with the same target decision rules using projected runner progress, then reevaluate when the ball is secured.                                                                                                                                  |
| RF's ball-backup role and secondary outfield jobs could disappear into generic holding.                          | Separate batted-ball backup, throw backup and supporting-base roles; actual targets drive compiled movement, including RF's transition after 2B fields.                                                                                                    |
| `HoldPosition` could describe a player while moving them toward a base.                                          | Deliberate holding has a stationary destination. Movement uses an explicit support, recovery, trailing, coverage or backup assignment.                                                                                                                     |
| A base's nominal receiver could displace a teammate already covering it after a relay.                           | Compare travel time among eligible conventional receivers, retain a ready middle infielder, and wait for the selected receiver before releasing a throw.                                                                                                   |
| 1B stayed committed to first after BR advanced beyond it.                                                        | Release first when no advance/return there remains and evaluate trailing only after higher-priority jobs.                                                                                                                                                  |
| Throw comparisons examined only a runner's next base and valued home mainly under prevent strategy.              | Compare every base the runner is attempting, including home before the runner reaches third. Use actual route/receiver readiness, force/tag timing, outs, score and inning. Home has strategic value in every strategy; an impossible home out cannot win. |
| Cutoff presence always implied an extra completed throw.                                                         | Distinguish direct, through-cutoff, relay, carry and cutoff-hold routes. A through-cutoff throw has no invented intermediate possession.                                                                                                                   |
| Backups were allocated too late or lost when the target changed.                                                 | Begin at contact; extend each actual throwing lane behind its receiving point, including an intermediate relay receiver; recompute supporting lanes at fielding, release, receipt, runner-base crossings and outs.                                         |
| An assignment could imply complete overthrow protection although the defender could not arrive before reception. | Record travel time and the receiving deadline. Prefer nearer eligible backups and expose late/unfulfilled coverage explicitly. Preserve a fixed reception deadline through a flight.                                                                       |
| Deep backup targets could extend through the schematic fence.                                                    | Reduce extension depth; when there is no usable space, use lateral support inside the field, leave receiving room, and report the space limitation.                                                                                                        |
| Assignment mutations after receipt could leave explanation/possession mismatches.                                | Snapshot complete regenerated assignments per phase; distinguish possession, release/follow-through and deliberate holding. English and Japanese use the same typed assignment reasons.                                                                    |

Throw ranking uses deterministic teaching heuristics, not win probabilities. A favorable tag/force estimate requires a timing margin above 0.05 seconds (0.8 under conservative strategy). Outside conservative strategy, an uncertain plate attempt is also available when the ball can arrive before the runner but the tag allowance makes the margin close. A ball predicted to arrive after the runner does not qualify for this exception. Home confidence uses the ball’s arrival lead before the runner, up to 0.6 seconds, rather than penalizing the fixed tag allowance twice. Competitive plate attempts are distinguished from favorable tag estimates. Available opportunities that prevent a run take priority over retiring a non-scoring runner; an inning-ending force, or a tag third out completed before the run, can also prevent it. A late/close game or explicit prevent strategy increases the value of a timely home play. With two outs, a reliable force third out receives extra value because it can cancel runs already across. The existing narrowly defined close attempt on BR at first remains available and is labeled by the close-play note.

A selected lead-runner throw can open an extra base for a trailing runner. The existing runner planner evaluates that opportunity without cancelling previously committed advances; retreat remains a separate reception decision. Existing runner speeds, legal basepaths, force chains, tags and third-out scoring remain in use.

## All-nine responsibility review

Each generated phase stores one active assignment per defender with target, reason, priority and replacement events. Having an assignment is not proof it is tactically correct; the tests below independently check expected jobs and physical behavior.

| Defender | Contact / pursuit                                                                         | Ball secured / throw                                                                       | Advancement / receipt / completion                                                               |
| -------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| P        | Read the receiving need; cover first if 1B fields, otherwise begin the applicable backup. | Back first, third or home on the actual lane when available.                               | Reallocate when target/coverage changes; stop live action at the third out.                      |
| C        | Protect a meaningful home threat; optional foul-side first support when safe.             | Receive home, support first, or direct play from home as assigned.                         | Return toward the catching position after support ends; do not claim possession during a flight. |
| 1B       | Cover first unless the primary fielder or the selected home cutoff.                       | Receive, cut/relay home, or maintain necessary first coverage.                             | Evaluate BR trailing outside the lane after first is released; retain higher-priority duties.    |
| 2B       | Field the right-side grounder, pivot/cover second, or enter the ball-side cutoff lane.    | Receive at the needed base or cut; compensate when 1B leaves first.                        | Keep a ready receiver in place and reevaluate on a relay/redirect.                               |
| SS       | Field the left-side grounder, cover second, or enter the selected cutoff lane.            | Cut to third or second from the left side; cover a vacated live base.                      | Retain second when already ready rather than wait for a distant nominal 2B.                      |
| 3B       | Cover a threatened third or deliberately guard the third-base side.                       | Receive a third-base throw; hold when no actual threat requires movement.                  | Update if a runner advances or retreats to third; avoid pursuing an already-fielded ball.        |
| LF       | Field/neighbor backup on the left; retain opposite-field support on right-side plays.     | Back a relevant actual throw/relay reception or support a likely second/third throw.       | Stop chasing the batted ball after possession and reassess the useful lane.                      |
| CF       | Back the adjacent outfielder or an infield/second-base play.                              | Transition to a throw/relay backup if it is the best available job.                        | Avoid duplicating every far-outfielder assignment; deliberate holding is allowed.                |
| RF       | Field/backup the right-side batted ball; support second on a left-field hit.              | Transition behind first after an infielder fields; support the selected second/relay lane. | Cover second if both middle infielders are occupied; update lanes after a changed target.        |

During contact the ball-pursuit assignment moves the primary fielder and backups begin moving. At fielding the engine recomputes all nine jobs. Throw release preserves its receiving lane and changes the thrower's explanation. Base crossings can release coverage or activate trailing without waiting for a throw to finish. Relay receipt can change the actual next receiver and movement targets. Completion/third out freezes further live action. Phase snapshots remain immutable when playback seeks backward.

A separate controlled runner-reaction test abandons home at relay reception and verifies actual `home → second` redirection, SS's ready coverage, all-nine reassignment, possession and movement continuity. The runner-intent change is explicitly supplied by the test: the current aggressive-runner heuristic would ordinarily continue that attempt. This is not represented as a naturally occurring result in all generated situations. The production planner's ordinary retreat rule is tested separately.

The wider new matrix covers **576** combinations: eight occupancies × three out counts × four ground/outfield locations × six target choices. The prior **168** base/out/location and **84** varied strength/speed/aggression/strategy/override configurations remain. Every phase is checked for unique all-nine assignments, meaningful reasons, translated explanations, stationary deliberate holds, movement toward the assigned destination within the speed limit, actual receiver arrival, throw-extension geometry, possession, valid scoring/state, and deterministic scrubbing.

Specific regressions also cover near-fence support, optional C support, RF emergency second coverage, timing-based backup selection, fixed receiving deadlines through flight, certain-to-score runners, and late/close-game versus secure-force decisions. The audit is a deterministic regression suite; it is not exhaustive tactical proof or external coach certification.

## Developer diagnostics

No diagnostics panel is added to the student interface. Run:

```sh
npm run audit:defense -- --list
npm run audit:defense -- --case ground-second --output /tmp/ground-second.json
npm run audit:defense -- --case aggressive-home --time 6 --output /tmp/home.json
npm run audit:defense -- --settings /tmp/custom-settings.json --time 4.5
```

`--settings` accepts the existing `DynamicSettings` JSON shape. Time uses playback seconds, including the one-second Ready phase; default time is the recorded BallFielded event. The command uses Vite's module loader without opening a listening server. `inspectDefense(scenario, time)` is also a pure developer API.

Output includes sampled game state, settings, ball destination/path/position/possession, primary fielder, selected target and reason, alternative targets with route/timing/margin/ranking reasons, current route, all nine assignments with sampled positions/targets/priorities/instructions, remaining distance, last reassessment, events and historical reassessments. `coverageIssues` distinguishes late backup, unfulfilled needs and limited space. Assignment travel/deadline estimates belong to the last assignment snapshot, not a claim of guaranteed arrival from an arbitrary scrubbed time. Historical target decisions and the current route are separate: after the throw finishes, a recorded preferred throw remains historical while the live route can be hold.

## Remaining limitations and coaching review

- Only the existing fair rolling ground balls and outfield hits are generated. Fly catches, tag-ups, bunts, errors, overthrows, rundowns and multi-stage double-cut relays are not implemented.
- Arm strengths, acceleration, reaction detail, footwork, tag reach and communication are simplified. All defenders share the configured abilities; ball return and aggression estimates are heuristics. A backup can correctly move yet arrive too late, which diagnostics report.
- Trailing uses geometric lateral separation and does not model bodies, obstruction adjudication or collision avoidance along every path. Coaches must review real-world spacing; this is not a physical safety simulator.
- The fence is the existing schematic boundary. A lateral-support alternative prevents a target beyond it; wall bounces, catchable ricochets, foul-ground dimensions and stadium obstacles are not modeled.
- The current runner heuristic deliberately does not model every decision to break from third on contact, nor every retreat/secondary advance after a redirect. An ordinary unforced R3 may hold even with the infield in; the defense does not invent a scoring attempt to demonstrate a home out. Forced R3, ordinary hit advancement, aggressive scoring attempts and two-out advances use the current rules.
- Only one intermediate relay is compiled. Emergency RF second coverage is supported and explicitly tested when the middle infielders are occupied; a fully generated two-middle-infielder double-cut system requires additional coordinated relay events.
- Unfulfilled coverage is exposed, not silently declared correct. The priority map ensures one active job per defender but cannot prove the best tactical plan for every continuous location. Team-specific cutoff depth, the optional catcher job, halfway alignment and risk preferences require coach review.

### Reproduced defects and reusable corrections

1. The contact forecast advanced runners but left defenders at starting positions. A hypothetical home throw was charged for 1B moving from first to a relay lane **after** fielding, even though 1B could begin that job at contact. Each candidate now projects supporting defenders at the configured speed under that candidate’s assignments, along with runner progress and the existing fielding-time runner reassessment. The selected contact plan drives actual movement; fielding then reevaluates using actual positions. This is a bounded contact estimate, not a search over every future runner/defender event.
2. A close home attempt was rejected as hopeless whenever the fixed tag allowance made its margin slightly negative. Outside conservative strategy, a ball that can arrive before the scoring runner now constitutes a **competitive**, uncertain plate attempt. Its diagnostics retain `viable: false`, `competitive: true`, the actual margin and the `competitive-home` reason. New English/Japanese narration does not promise an out.
3. Margin-only ranking could prefer an easy out on BR at second while conceding the run, even under prevent strategy. The candidate now records `preventsRun` and ranks available run-saving opportunities ahead of non-scoring-runner outs. This flag describes the consequence of making the out; the separate `viable`/`competitive` flags determine whether the opportunity is available. A force third out can cancel the run; a non-force third out only prevents it if the expected **tag**, including waiting for the runner to reach the base, precedes scoring. A catch at second is not itself a tag out.
4. Only cutoff/relay receivers reconsidered a subsequent throw. An ordinary base receiver now checks for one additional useful automatic throw on an outfield play, immediately after any tag resolution. It recalculates actual coverage/backups and does not insert an extra reading pause before the transfer. This is bounded to one additional base-directed throw; it does not introduce arbitrary throw chains, rundowns or additional intermediate relays. Coach-selected hold/cutoff-and-hold and target overrides retain their semantics.

For the exact hard-hit, aggressive, **fast-runner** example with R2, both standard and prevent strategies now prepare home and complete `CF → 1B → C`. The fixed model puts the ball at the plate approximately 0.20 seconds before the runner; the 0.25-second tag allowance makes this a close attempt rather than a guaranteed out. The illustrated run can still score. Changing the tag/out result merely to demonstrate a successful play would conceal the timing limitation.

At the same coordinate with normal contact and an average aggressive runner, the engine can instead return `CF → SS`, then immediately challenge home `SS → C` when the new receiving position creates a competitive chance. A hard-hit average-speed runner who holds third is not converted into a scoring attempt to force a home throw. If a run has already scored, the defense can challenge BR elsewhere rather than make a hopeless throw home.

The regression suite adds the named `center-home-close` fixture, all-nine checks at nine nearby coordinates under standard/prevent strategies and different outs, explicit competitive-versus-favorable timing, run-consequence comparisons, the ordinary-base-receiver follow-up, conservative/hold overrides, and bilingual production-browser replay. Existing speed, possession, scoring, seek and tactical-matrix checks remain required.

The production build passes; the expanded dynamic browser suite verifies both `CF → 1B → C` and `CF → SS → C` plus the new Japanese close-play explanation.
